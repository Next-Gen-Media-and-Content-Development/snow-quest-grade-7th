Q2 Why does the torch lamp glow in one position of its switch? ▼

Step 1: Identify what the switch does. A switch either completes the electric circuit (ON position) or breaks it (OFF position). Step 2: Explain the ON position. When the switch is ON, it connects the cell/battery terminals to the lamp through wires/metal strips inside the torch. This makes a complete (closed) path for electric current. Step 3: Explain the OFF position. When the switch is OFF, it creates a gap in the path. The circuit becomes incomplete (open), so current cannot flow. Step 4: Link to the glow. The lamp glows only when current flows through its filament (or through LED circuitry). Hence it glows in the ON position and not in the OFF position.

Q3 What do you find inside a torchlight after opening it? Name the main parts related to the circuit. ▼

Step 1: Observe the cell compartment. Most torchlights contain two or more electric cells placed end-to-end. Step 2: Identify the lamp. At the front, there is a lamp (incandescent bulb or LED arrangement) which produces light. Step 3: Identify the switch. A switch is provided to turn the circuit ON or OFF. Step 4: Identify connecting parts. Inside the torch there are metal strips/springs and wires that connect: 1. The cells/battery to the lamp 2. The switch into the circuit so that it can open/close the circuit Step 5: Conclude. The main circuit-related parts are: cells (or battery), lamp, switch, and connecting conductors (metal strips/wires/springs).

Q4 How can you identify the positive and negative terminals of an electric cell? ▼

Step 1: Look for symbols. The cell has (+) and (–) signs marked on its body. Step 2: Observe the ends of the cell. One end has a small protruding metal cap; the other end has a flat metal disc. Step 3: Match each end with the terminal. 1. The protruding metal cap is the positive (+) terminal. 2. The flat metal disc is the negative (–) terminal. Step 4: Conclude. Thus, by the sign marks and by the shape (cap and disc), we can identify the terminals correctly.

Q5 In a torch, why are two or more cells placed in a particular order? What happens if one cell is reversed? ▼

Step 1: State how cells must be connected for a battery. In a battery, the positive terminal of one cell must connect to the negative terminal of the next cell (series connection). Step 2: Explain why a particular order is needed. This arrangement adds the effects of the cells, giving a higher potential difference (voltage) and allowing the lamp to get sufficient energy. Step 3: Explain what happens if one cell is reversed. If one cell is reversed, terminals may become like-to-like in the chain (positive to positive or negative to negative). This disturbs the series connection, reducing or cancelling the effective voltage. Step 4: Result in the circuit. With less or no effective voltage, current may not flow properly and the lamp may glow dimly or not glow at all. Step 5: Conclude. Hence, cells must be placed in the correct order so that positive of one touches negative of the next.

Q6 What is a battery? Explain using the arrangement of cells in a torch. ▼

Step 1: Start with the definition. A battery is a combination of two or more electric cells connected together. Step 2: Describe the torch arrangement. In a torch, cells are placed so that: 1. The positive terminal of the first cell touches the negative terminal of the second cell. 2. This pattern continues if more cells are used. Step 3: Explain the purpose. By combining cells, the battery can: 1. Provide more energy to the circuit 2. Run the device for a longer time and/or make it work properly Step 4: Conclude. Therefore, the set of cells connected in this manner inside a torch is called a battery.

Q7 In an incandescent lamp, which part glows and why? Describe its terminals. ▼

Step 1: Identify the glowing part. The thin wire inside the glass bulb glows when the lamp is switched on. Step 2: Name the glowing part. This thin wire is called the filament. Step 3: Explain why it glows. When electric current passes through the filament, it gets very hot due to resistance and produces light (incandescence). Step 4: Describe how the filament is connected. The filament is attached to two thicker supporting wires. Step 5: Identify the two terminals. 1. One terminal is the metal case on the side of the lamp base. 2. The other terminal is the metal tip at the centre of the base. Step 6: Important point. These terminals are fixed so that they do not touch each other; otherwise the circuit would not pass through the filament. Step 7: Conclude. Thus, the filament glows due to heating by current, and the two separate terminals allow current to enter and leave the filament.

Q8 How is an LED different from an incandescent lamp? How can you identify the LED terminals? ▼

Step 1: Compare internal construction. 1. Incandescent lamp has a filament inside a glass bulb. 2. LED (Light Emitting Diode) does not have a filament. Step 2: Compare working idea (basic level). 1. Incandescent lamp produces light because the filament gets hot and glows. 2. LED produces light without heating a filament (it is an electronic device). Step 3: Identify LED terminals. An LED has two terminals connected to two wires (leads): 1. The longer lead is the positive (+) terminal. 2. The shorter lead is the negative (–) terminal. Step 4: Conclude. LEDs are identified by the absence of filament and by unequal lead lengths for terminals.

Q1 Define an electric circuit. Differentiate between an open circuit and a closed circuit with suitable examples. ▼

Step 1: Define electric circuit. An electric circuit is a complete and continuous path through which electric current can flow from the source (cell/battery) through components (like lamp) and back to the source. Step 2: Explain a closed circuit. A closed circuit is a circuit in which the path is complete (no gap). Result: Current flows and the device works. Example: Torch switch ON → lamp glows. Step 3: Explain an open circuit. An open circuit is a circuit in which the path is broken due to a gap (for example, switch OFF, loose wire, or broken filament). Result: Current does not flow and the device does not work. Example: Torch switch OFF → lamp does not glow. Step 4: Write the difference clearly. 1. Closed circuit: complete path; current flows; bulb glows. 2. Open circuit: broken path; current stops; bulb does not glow.

Mark + at the cell cap and – at the flat end. 2. Ensure wires connect from one terminal of the cell → switch → lamp → back to the other terminal. Step 5: Final check. Your ON diagram must be a complete loop; your OFF diagram must have a visible break at the switch.

Q3 Explain the structure of an electric cell. How are its terminals identified? ▼

Step 1: State what an electric cell is. An electric cell is a portable source of electrical energy used in many devices. Step 2: Explain terminals. Every cell has two terminals: 1. Positive terminal (+) 2. Negative terminal (–) Step 3: Explain physical identification. 1. The end with the small protruding metal cap is the positive terminal. 2. The end with the flat metal disc is the negative terminal. Step 4: Explain symbol identification. The cell body usually has (+) and (–) signs printed to indicate terminals. Step 5: Conclude. Thus, by shape (cap/disc) and markings (+/–), the terminals of a cell can be identified correctly.

Q4 What is a battery? Why do we use a battery instead of a single cell in many devices like a torch? ▼

Step 1: Define battery. A battery is a combination of two or more electric cells connected together, usually in series (positive of one to negative of the next). Step 2: Explain why a battery is used. Many devices need: 1. More potential difference (voltage) to operate properly 2. More energy so that the device can run for longer time Step 3: Connect to the torch example. A torch often needs two or more cells so that the lamp gets enough energy to glow brightly. Step 4: Conclude. Therefore, a battery is used instead of a single cell to provide sufficient energy/voltage and longer operation.

Q5 A torch does not glow after new cells are inserted. List the possible mistakes related to cell placement and explain how to correct them. ▼

Step 1: Check cell orientation. Possible mistake: One or both cells inserted in reverse direction. Correction: Place cells so that the positive terminal of one touches the negative terminal of the next (as shown in the torch diagram). Step 2: Check series connection order. Possible mistake: Terminals not making proper end-to-end contact due to wrong arrangement. Correction: Ensure the protruding cap (+) of one cell is in contact with the flat end (–) of the other. Step 3: Check contact points. Possible mistake: Cells not touching the spring/metal strip properly. Correction: Press and place cells firmly so springs and metal strips touch the terminals. Step 4: Check for mixing old and new cells. Possible issue: One weak/old cell reduces effective output. Correction: Use cells of same type and similar condition (preferably new). Step 5: Conclude. Most torch failures after inserting cells are due to reversed terminals or poor contact; correct orientation and firm contact usually solves it.

Q6 Describe an incandescent lamp used in a torch. How are its two terminals arranged so that they do not touch each other? ▼

Step 1: Identify main parts. An incandescent torch lamp has: 1. A glass bulb 2. A thin filament wire inside 3. Two thicker supporting wires 4. A metal base with two separate terminals Step 2: Explain filament connection. The filament is attached to two thicker wires that support it. Step 3: Explain the two terminals. 1. One supporting wire connects to the metal case (outer metal shell) of the lamp base. 2. The other supporting wire connects to the metal tip at the centre of the base. Step 4: Explain how they are kept separate. Insulating material is used between the metal case and the metal tip so that they do not touch. Step 5: Why separation is important. If the terminals touched, current would bypass the filament and the filament would not glow. Step 6: Conclude. Thus, separate terminals ensure current passes through the filament, heating it to produce light.

Q7 Write any four differences between an LED lamp and an incandescent lamp (torch bulb). ▼

Step 1: Difference in filament. Incandescent: Has a filament. LED: No filament. Step 2: Difference in how light is produced (school level). Incandescent: Filament gets hot and glows. LED: Produces light electronically without a glowing filament. Step 3: Difference in identification of terminals. Incandescent: Terminals are metal case and metal tip at base. LED: Terminals are two leads; longer lead is + and shorter is –. Step 4: Difference in energy use. Incandescent: Wastes more energy as heat. LED: Uses less electricity for same brightness (more efficient). Step 5: Difference in durability. Incandescent: Filament can break easily. LED: Generally lasts longer because there is no filament to break. Step 6: Conclude. These differences explain why many modern torches use LEDs instead of incandescent bulbs.

Q8 Why is it dangerous to perform experiments using the mains power supply at home or school? What precautions are suggested in the chapter? ▼

Step 1: Explain why mains electricity is dangerous. Mains supply provides high voltage and can cause: 1. Severe electric shock 2. Burns 3. Fire due to short circuit It can even be fatal if mishandled. Step 2: Mention danger signs. Danger signs on poles and appliances warn that electricity can be harmful if not handled carefully. Step 3: Give the chapter’s precautions. 1. Never perform experiments with power supply from sockets at home or school. 2. Even portable generators can be dangerous; avoid using them for student experiments. 3. Use only batteries/cells (like those in torches, wall clocks, radios, remotes) for experiments. 4. Perform activities with teacher/elder supervision, especially when opening devices like torches. Step 4: Conclude. Following these precautions keeps experiments safe and prevents accidents.

Q9 Activity-based: You are given one cell, one bulb (incandescent), and two wires. Explain step-by-step how you will make the bulb glow. ▼

Step 1: Identify the terminals. 1. Cell has + (metal cap) and – (flat disc). 2. Bulb has two terminals: metal case and metal tip at the base. Step 2: Make the first connection. Connect one wire from the positive terminal of the cell to the metal tip (or metal case) of the bulb. Step 3: Make the second connection to complete the circuit. Connect the second wire from the negative terminal of the cell to the remaining terminal of the bulb (whichever was not connected in Step 2). Step 4: Ensure firm contact. Hold wires tightly on metal parts (not on glass or insulation) so that current can pass. Step 5: Observe. If the circuit is complete (closed), current flows and the filament glows, so the bulb lights up. Step 6: Troubleshooting if it does not glow. 1. Check that wires touch only metal parts. 2. Ensure bulb is not fused (filament not broken). 3. Ensure cell is not exhausted. Step 7: Conclude. A bulb glows only when both its terminals are connected to the two terminals of the cell to form a closed circuit.

Q10 What is the role of a switch in a circuit? Explain with a torch example. ▼

Step 1: Define the role. A switch is a device used to open or close an electric circuit. Step 2: Explain ‘switch ON’. When ON, the switch closes the circuit (completes the path), allowing current to flow. Torch example: ON → lamp glows. Step 3: Explain ‘switch OFF’. When OFF, the switch opens the circuit (creates a gap), stopping current. Torch example: OFF → lamp does not glow. Step 4: Conclude. Thus, a switch controls the flow of current by making or breaking the circuit.

Q11 While connecting wires to make a circuit, why is about 1 cm plastic covering removed from wire ends? What happens if it is not removed? ▼

Step 1: State what the plastic covering is. The plastic covering is an insulator; it does not allow electric current to pass through it. Step 2: Explain why it is removed. Removing about 1 cm of plastic exposes the metal conductor inside the wire. This exposed metal can make direct contact with cell terminals and lamp terminals, allowing current to flow. Step 3: What happens if it is not removed. If the plastic is not removed, the wire end remains insulated. Result: No proper electrical contact → circuit remains open → bulb will not glow. Step 4: Conclude. Therefore, removing insulation at the ends is necessary to complete the circuit.
