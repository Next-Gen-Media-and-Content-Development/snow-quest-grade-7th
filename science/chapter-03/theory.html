Electricity and circuits: a quick start

Electricity becomes useful when it travels in a complete path called a circuit!

Electricity is the flow of electric charge, and an electric circuit is a closed loop that allows this charge to move from a source, through components, and back again. If the path is broken anywhere, the circuit is open and current cannot flow.

In real life, big power stations (like hydroelectric plants) generate electricity, but inside our homes and gadgets the same basic idea works: a source provides energy, wires provide a path, and devices (like bulbs or buzzers) use that energy to do a job.

So, to make any circuit work you need a source, a conducting path, and a component that uses electricity—plus a closed loop.

Cells and batteries: the power source

A tiny cell can start a whole chain of energy changes in a circuit!

An electric cell provides the push (potential difference) that makes charges move. It has two terminals: positive (+) and negative (−). When connected in a circuit, current flows from the positive terminal through the circuit and returns to the negative terminal.

A battery is a combination of two or more cells, but in everyday language we often call even a single cell a “battery” (like in many phones). Using more cells can provide a stronger push and help devices work better.

Always connect cells correctly: the arrangement of terminals matters, and wrong connections may stop components from working.

🧠 Try It Yourself

1. What are the two terminals of a cell called?
✅ Positive terminal and negative terminal.
2. What does a cell provide to a circuit?
✅ Electrical energy (a potential difference) that drives current.
3. What is a battery in science terms?
✅ A combination of two or more cells connected together.
4. Why might a device use two cells instead of one?
✅ To get a larger potential difference so it works properly.
5. In which direction is current taken to flow in a circuit?
✅ From the positive terminal to the negative terminal (conventional current).

Electric lamps: incandescent bulb and LED

Two lamps can light up a room in very different ways—one heats up, the other shines smartly!

An incandescent lamp glows because a thin wire called a filament becomes very hot when current passes through it. The filament is enclosed in a glass bulb. If the filament breaks, the lamp is said to be fused, and current cannot pass through the broken part.

An LED (Light Emitting Diode) glows without a hot filament. It allows current mainly in one direction, so its connection matters. If an LED does not glow, one common reason is that it is connected with wrong polarity.

Both lamps change electrical energy into light, but LEDs are generally more efficient and produce less heat.

🧠 Try It Yourself

1. What part of an incandescent bulb glows?
✅ The filament.
2. Why does a fused bulb not glow?
✅ Its filament is broken, so the circuit is open.
3. What does LED stand for?
✅ Light Emitting Diode.
4. Why might an LED not glow even with cells connected?
✅ It may be connected in the wrong direction (wrong polarity).
5. Which lamp usually wastes less energy as heat: LED or incandescent?
✅ LED.

      Switches and making a simple on off control

      A switch is like a gatekeeper—deciding when electricity may pass!

      A switch controls a circuit by making or breaking the conducting path. When the switch is ON, it closes the circuit and current can flow. When it is OFF, it opens the circuit and stops current.

      A simple switch can be made using pins and a safety pin or paper clip: when metal touches metal, the path becomes complete; when they separate, the path breaks. This shows that switching is really about connecting and disconnecting conductors.

      Switches are used everywhere—torches, fans, doorbells, and even inside appliances—because controlling current safely is essential.

      ✅ Closed circuit.

      ✅ The circuit opens and the bulb goes off.
      4. Name one material feature needed for switch contacts.
      ✅ They should be conductors (usually metal).
      5. Why is a switch important for safety?
      ✅ It lets us stop current when not needed or during danger.

      Circuit diagrams and standard symbols

      Circuit symbols are the secret language that lets engineers and students understand the same circuit!

      Instead of drawing real-looking pictures of cells, bulbs, and wires, we use circuit symbols to make neat circuit diagrams. This saves time and avoids confusion, especially in complicated circuits.

      In a cell symbol, the long line shows the positive terminal and the short line shows the negative terminal. In an LED symbol, the triangle shows the direction in which current can flow, and arrows show that light is emitted.

      Standard symbols are made by organizations like IEC, ANSI, and IEEE. Using the same symbols worldwide helps people communicate clearly across countries and industries.

      Wires, conductors and insulators

      The right wire material is like the right road—smooth travel for electric current!

      Wires in circuits are usually made of metals because metals are good conductors: they allow electric current to pass through easily. If we used many non-metals, the current would be very small or may not flow at all.

      Materials that allow current to pass are called conductors (like copper and aluminium). Materials that do not allow current to pass easily are called insulators (like plastic, rubber, wood, and glass).

      Electric wires are covered with plastic or rubber insulation to prevent electric shocks and to stop unwanted contact between wires, which could cause short circuits.

      🧠 Try It Yourself

      1. Why are wires usually made of metal?
      ✅ Because metals are good conductors of electricity.
      2. Give two examples of conductors.
      ✅ Copper and aluminium.
      3. Give two examples of insulators.
      ✅ Plastic and rubber.
      4. Why are wires coated with plastic or rubber?
      ✅ To prevent shocks and avoid short circuits.
      5. What will happen if two bare wires touch in a circuit?
      ✅ It may cause a short circuit and the device may not work safely.
